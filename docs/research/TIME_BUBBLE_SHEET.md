# The time-bubble sheet: what physics allows, and what the family already holds

Owner's proposal (USER-STATED, 2026-10-05): oreality can be represented by arealities in prealities in infinite-time finite spaces; a time bubble whose boundary is a sheet of infinitesimally thin singularities, like a bubble's film, lets a finite space exist infinitely long inside itself and lets computational information cross the sheet. `[HYPER]` Retrieved 2026-10-05 unless marked *recalled*.

## Four distinct claims inside it

1. **The film is a singularity.** In general relativity a thin shell is not one. The Israel (Darmois–Israel) junction conditions keep the metric continuous across the shell and let the extrinsic curvature jump; the jump equals the shell's surface stress-energy (Lanczos equation) ([overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/darmois-israel-junction-formalism)). Curvature is a delta function, finite surface energy, and geodesics cross it. A genuine singular sheet, with divergent curvature and a degenerate metric, would not carry a signal through at all. So "sheet" should mean a regular thin shell, or a horizon. `[FORM]` for the first; the rest is the proposal.
2. **Finite space, infinite time.** The real instance is the de Sitter static patch: a horizon of finite area, so finitely many states, and unbounded static time (*recalled*; Poincaré recurrence time of order `e^S`). Bounds: Bekenstein `S ≤ 2πER/(ħc)`; covariant (Bousso) bound, entropy on a non-expanding light sheet of a surface is at most a quarter of its area ([entropy bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0203101), [Bousso](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5635)). Finite states plus infinite time gives recurrence, not new computation: the finite-state verdict of `wiki/hypersphere-and-preality.md`. `[FORM]`
3. **Infinite interior time against finite exterior time.** Redshift cannot supply it. A clock deeper in a potential runs slower than one outside, so a shell with a flat interior has interior time elapsing *less*. The needed asymmetry has to come from causal structure: the exterior's time ends or is cut off (pinch-off, horizon) while the interior goes on. That is a baby universe, a small throat to a large inflating interior (Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov, Farhi–Guth, *recalled*), with no return. `[FRAME]`
4. **"Arrive right after we left."** This needs the interior's infinite history to be readable at one exterior event. Two models, with different strength:
   * Malament–Hogarth (MH) spacetimes: an event whose past contains a worldline of infinite elapsed time, so an observer can receive the outcome of an infinite computation in finite time ([SEP, Supertasks](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-supertasks/)). Etesi and Németi analysed a subclass containing rotating (Kerr) black holes and showed relations beyond Turing computability can be decided ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0104023)). Whether physically realizable (stability, signal strength) is contested. This is the mechanism for "translating information across the sheet": the receiver crosses at the MH event.
   * Closed timelike curves: polynomial-size CTC computers have exactly the power of PSPACE, classical or quantum ([Aaronson–Watrous](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.2669)). That is "arrive before you left" but not infinite-time power.

So the infinite-time-computer reading and the "return right after" reading use different resources (MH limit stage versus CTC fixed point). `[FORM]` on each model's stated result; which one the sheet is `[OPEN]`.

## What crosses, and how much

Whatever the mechanism, the bits that cross a sheet of area `A` per use are bounded by the area bound (about `A/4` in Planck units) on any non-expanding light sheet (Bousso, as above). ## Quantum computation in infinite time, and recurrence (USER-STATED 2026-10-05, then analysed)

Stated: the sheet "allows us to determine recurrent computations with quantum computation in infinite time." `[HYPER]` Three readings, kept apart.

* **R1, recurrence is guaranteed.** For unitary evolution on a finite-dimensional space, the state returns within any `ε` of its start infinitely often (quantum recurrence, *recalled*; the same almost-periodicity the numeric check uses). Infinite time supplies it with no further machinery. What infinite time adds is that the return can be waited for however long it takes; the exterior learning of it needs the hand-off of the previous section. `[FORM]`
* **R2, the limit stage is a recurrence detector.** The infinite-time Turing machine's rule at a limit stage is limsup per cell: a cell is 1 at `ω` exactly when it is 1 cofinally often, that is, when it recurs. For a quantum interior the analogue needs a rule, and a canonical one exists: the **time average** of the state, which for a finite-dimensional unitary converges to the diagonal ensemble `Σ_E P_E ρ P_E` (dephasing in the energy eigenspaces; mean ergodic theorem). This closes the open item "which limit rule at the limit stage" for a finite-dimensional quantum interior: Cesàro, which gives the dephased state. `scripts/check_quantum_recurrence.py` (REPRODUCED 2026-10-05, standard library, random 4-level spectrum): the largest off-diagonal entry of the average is `2.6e-3`, `3.5e-4`, `9.7e-5` at `10²`, `10³`, `10⁴` steps. Other rules (cluster set of the orbit, which is the whole orbit-closure torus, or the ordinal-remainder rule that returns the start) give different values; the choice is a postulate, now with a physically natural default. `[FRAME]`
* **R3, deciding recurrent computations.** For an interior with unbounded memory, deciding what recurs is what the infinite-time machine already does, classically. Finite-precision quantum computation is simulable by a classical machine with exponential overhead, so quantum adds no computability beyond classical in the same time model (standard, *recalled*). What quantum adds is **state-space size**: `n` qubits are a `2^n`-dimensional space, so an `S`-bit finite interior has a Hilbert space of dimension about `e^S`. The ε-return time on random spectra grows with dimension (same script, `ε = 0.1`, seven draws, medians of the first-return step: 1 for `d=2`, 2 for `d=3`, 14 for `d=4`, 49 for `d=5`; small sample, heuristic scaling `ε^-(d-1)` from a torus of dimension `d-1`). Extrapolated to `d ≈ e^S` that is doubly exponential in `S` (`[HYPER]`, not checked). Infinite time makes such waits free, which is the only sense in which quantum plus infinite time exceeds quantum in finite time. `[OPEN]` whether that is the owner's claim.

Limits: the check is one toy spectrum family; degenerate or rational spectra recur exactly and early; an interior with continuous observation (measurement) is not unitary and breaks R1.

## The interior entangled with the outside (USER-STATED 2026-10-05, then analysed)

Stated: "quantum entangled to outside." `[HYPER]` Four consequences, the first two checked on a toy (`scripts/check_entangled_interior.py`, REPRODUCED 2026-10-05, standard library, 2×2 interior and exterior, diagonal joint Hamiltonian, entangled initial state).

* **Entanglement alone carries nothing.** With no interaction across the sheet, a unitary applied to the interior leaves the exterior's reduced state unchanged at every later time (change `0.0000` over the sampled times). This is no-signaling. `[FORM]`
* **Interaction is the channel.** With an interaction term the same interior unitary changes the exterior's state (change `0.3931`). So whatever crosses the sheet crosses because the sheet couples the two sides; entanglement correlates, coupling transmits. Teleportation of an interior result needs a classical message, which is the Malament–Hogarth crossing of the previous section (*recalled* from the standard protocol, not run here). `[FORM]` on the toy, `[FRAME]` on the mapping to the sheet.
* **Recurrence now belongs to the joint system.** The finite-dimensional recurrence theorem and the Cesàro/diagonal-ensemble limit (previous section) apply to interior plus exterior. The interior's reduced state is the partial trace of the joint diagonal ensemble, generally mixed. Toy: with the interaction on, the coherence magnitude `|ρ_in[0][1](t)|` fluctuates (spread `0.1274`); with it off it is constant (spread `0.0000`). A correction made while checking: the Cesàro average of that off-diagonal entry is about zero in **both** cases (`0.0001`, `0.0000`), since coherence rotates in the energy basis, so it cannot be the test. If the exterior is effectively infinite-dimensional, the joint recurrence time is unbounded and the reduced interior state relaxes without recurring, which ends R1 for the interior alone. `[FORM]` for the finite toy, `[HYPER]` for the infinite exterior.
* **Bound.** Entanglement entropy between the interior and the outside is at most about the sheet area over four (area law, Bousso/Ryu–Takayanagi, *recalled*). So the sheet's finite area caps how entangled the infinite-time interior can be with the outside, while its internal Hilbert space may be larger.
* **Black-hole analogue.** Information inside is recoverable from outside through entanglement: Page curve, entanglement islands, and the Hayden–Preskill protocol, where a message thrown into an old black hole is decodable from about as many extra radiation qubits as the message has, after the scrambling time ([overview of the protocol and island picture](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.09144), [Hayden–Preskill in circuit models](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2024-08-08-1434/)). That is the closest established physics to "the interior is entangled with the outside and its content can be read there"; it is for evaporating black holes in a semiclassical treatment, not for the owner's sheet. `[FRAME]`

## Closest to wormhole interiors (USER-STATED 2026-10-05, then mapped)

Stated: the sheet "feels closest to black holes, but more like wormhole interiors." `[HYPER]` The mapping below is mine; each cited result is for AdS/CFT-type models, not for the owner's sheet.

* **Entanglement as the bridge (ER=EPR).** Two entangled black holes in the thermofield double state are connected by an Einstein–Rosen bridge (Maldacena–Susskind; [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ER_=_EPR)). The interior as a wormhole throat to a second region is the interior-entangled-with-outside picture of the previous section made geometric. `[FRAME]`
* **Coupling makes it traversable.** Gao–Jafferis–Wall: a deformation that couples the two boundaries generates negative null energy and makes the wormhole traversable, and the construction is related to a teleportation protocol on a thermofield double ([paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1608.05687), [bulk view of teleportation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP08(2018)140)). This is the toy's result in the previous section (no coupling: no signal; coupling: signal) in its gravitational form: the sheet is a traversable wormhole exactly if it is a coupling. `[FRAME]` Without the coupling the Einstein–Rosen bridge is not traversable.
* **Finite mouth, long interior.** The bridge's volume grows linearly with time (complexity equals volume, [Stanford–Susskind](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2678), [Susskind](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0690)), while the mouth area, and so the entropy `S`, is fixed. That is the owner's finite exterior with a growing interior, and it is the time-dependent version of the earlier "TARDIS" geometry. `[FRAME]`
* **Where the doubly exponential recurrence comes from.** The earlier extrapolation (return time doubly exponential in `S`, flagged `[HYPER]`) is the Brown–Susskind picture: complexity grows linearly for an exponentially long time, saturates near `e^S`, stays maximal, and returns to a small value at times `exp(exp(S))`. A rigorous proof exists for a random local circuit model ([Saturation and recurrence of quantum complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09734)); for black holes it remains a conjecture. So the extrapolation is **DOCUMENTED** for this model class, no longer only mine, and the bridge's volume is what recurs. `[FRAME]`
* **What does not map.** A wormhole's interior is a second asymptotic region of the same theory, not a nested bubble inside a finite exterior volume; the infinite-time property (state recurrence in a finite space) belongs to the boundary theory, while the bridge picture gives a bulk geometry that grows for `e^S` and then, in this model, shrinks. Infinite interior *volume* is not a feature of these bridges (it is at most `e^S`-ish). The owner's infinite-volume interior (open Coleman–De Luccia slices) and the wormhole bridge are two different candidates. `[OPEN]` Which one the sheet is.
* **Return.** A traversable wormhole connecting the interior to the outside would let an occupant return without a closed timelike curve, at the cost of the coupling and the negative energy. Whether the return lands "right after we left" in exterior time depends on the geometry's time shift and is `[OPEN]`; sufficiently asymmetric wormholes can be turned into time machines (Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever, *recalled*), which is the closed timelike curve branch with its PSPACE bound.
