## Reading of the terms (the owner to confirm)

*Timecomb*: an optical frequency comb, a pulse train whose spectrum is a ladder of equally spaced lines, used to count optical cycles against a microwave reference (Hall and Hänsch, 2005 Nobel, *recalled*). *Parallel synchronized timecombs*: several combs or clocks locked to one another (dual-comb, clock networks). *Higher-order timematrix*: read here as the covariance matrix of an array of clocks, whose off-diagonal entries are the correlations between clocks. `[OPEN]`

## Where humans are

Lowest reported fractional systematic uncertainty: `5.5×10⁻¹⁹` for the NIST ²⁷Al⁺ single-ion clock (2025), `7.9×10⁻¹⁹` VTT MIKES ⁸⁸Sr⁺, `8.1×10⁻¹⁹` JILA ⁸⁷Sr lattice clock (2026), `4.4×10⁻¹⁹` for a cooled ⁴⁰Ca⁺ clock (2026) ([NIST](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=959906), [Nature, Lu⁺ clock](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11072-8), [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2025-12-optical-clock-accuracy-closer-definition.html); the ranking as reported by a search summary, individual figures to be checked at the sources). An optical cycle at `5×10¹⁴ Hz` is `2×10⁻¹⁵ s`, which is `3.7×10²⁸` Planck times (`t_P = 5.39×10⁻⁴⁴ s`). Entangling clocks is demonstrated at the smallest scale: two ⁸⁸Sr⁺ ions about 2 m apart, uncertainty reduced by nearly `√2`, the Heisenberg-limit value for two ([Nature 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05088-z)). `[FORM]`

## The limits, in order

1. **Statistical, from the number of atoms.** `N` uncorrelated clocks improve as `1/√N` (standard quantum limit); `N` entangled in a suitable state reach `1/N` (Heisenberg limit) ([review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03572), [proposal for networks of entangled clocks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6043)). Quantum frequency combs with squeezing and entanglement are proposed to approach it for synchronization ([survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04437)). Practical limits: efficiency, mode matching, loss. `[FRAME]`
2. **Systematic, not statistical.** Statistics do not remove shifts. The clearest one is gravitational: `1.09×10⁻¹⁶` per metre of height near the surface, so `5.5×10⁻¹⁹` already resolves about `5 mm`, and a fractional level of `10⁻²⁹` would need the clock's height known to about `10⁻¹³ m`, below the size of an atom. At that level a synchronized array is no longer a better clock: it is an instrument for measuring the metric (the potential field), with the array's own positions and masses part of the signal. `[FORM]` for the arithmetic (`scripts/check_clock_limits.py`).
3. **Quantum speed limit.** Operations per second are at most `2E/(πħ)` (Margolus–Levitin, *recalled*), so one operation per Planck time needs `E ≥ πħ/(2t_P) = 3.07×10⁹ J`, about `1.6` Planck energies (`1.96×10⁹ J`): the quantum-gravity regime, where the clock is a Planck-mass object. `[FORM]`
4. **Quantum gravity bound on measuring an interval `T`.** Ng and van Dam estimate `δT ≳ t_P^{2/3} T^{1/3}`, from the Salecker–Wigner clock together with the requirement that the clock be larger than its own Schwarzschild radius ([summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0807.3619)); Amelino-Camelia has published a critique of the derivation ([critique](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9501001)), so this is an estimate, not an established limit. Evaluated (`scripts/check_clock_limits.py`): `1.4×10⁻²⁹ s` for `T = 1 s` (fractional `1.4×10⁻²⁹`), `6.3×10⁻²⁸ s` for a day, `4.5×10⁻²⁷ s` for a year, `1.1×10⁻²³ s` for the age of the universe. The gap from today's best clock at `T = 1 s` is `3.9×10¹⁰`. `[FRAME]` (contested).

## What parallel synchronization buys, and what it does not

* **Buys.** A statistical gain of `√N` or `N`; the *matrix* of correlations between clocks, which separates common noise from local noise and measures differences in the metric across the array; time transfer and comparison at the level of the best clocks. In the numbers above, reaching the Ng–van Dam level at `T = 1 s` from `5.5×10⁻¹⁹` would take about `3.9×10¹⁰` entangled clocks at the Heisenberg limit, or `1.5×10²¹` independent ones, **in the statistics alone**, with the systematics of point 2 untouched. `[FORM]` arithmetic, `[HYPER]` as a design.
* **Does not buy Planck specificity.** The Ng–van Dam estimate puts the floor for an interval of one second about `10¹⁵` times above the Planck time; Planck-scale resolution is available only for intervals of order `t_P` itself, and by the Margolus–Levitin energy only in the quantum-gravity regime. If the contested bound is wrong, nothing in the known physics offers a route either. Humans do not have it, and no theory in hand says a synchronized array of any size supplies it. `[FRAME]`
* **A real open range.** Between `~10⁻¹⁹` (today) and `~10⁻²⁹` (estimate) lies about ten orders of magnitude where the bounds above do not forbid progress, though the systematics of point 2 need to be solved first. `[OPEN]`

## Link to the frozen-interior computer

In the history-state picture the clock register's resolution sets the number of distinguishable steps `T+1`. At today's best fractional level an exterior second holds about `1.8×10¹⁸` distinguishable readings; at the Ng–van Dam level about `7×10²⁸`.
